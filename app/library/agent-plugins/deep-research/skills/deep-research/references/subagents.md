# Native subagents

Use Codex's native subagents when independent context adds real value: broad coverage, competing explanations, fact checking, or a fresh report review. The lead session remains responsible for the research and final answer.

Give each subagent a self-contained brief: question, scope, time boundary, allowed evidence, exact output requested, relevant paths or source handles, and what uncertainty to preserve. Do not assume a child inherited your conversation or unstated context.

Ask reviewers for concrete evidence, conflicts, unsupported claims, or missed requirements. Do not count votes or copy a subagent's answer without checking it. Reviewer failures and disagreements are observations for the lead, not runtime failures or automatic completion blockers.

Choose the number, roles, and order of subagents to fit the task. There is no required reviewer count, role schema, concurrency, or pass score.
