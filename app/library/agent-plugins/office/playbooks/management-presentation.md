# Management Reporting

## Narrative

A manager who reads only the page titles should understand how the period went, why it went that way, and what decision or action is required. State status or judgment in the title instead of using only a topic label.

Use the opening page to show the overall state of the period, the most important supplied outcome, and the largest gap or decision. Organize the rest of the deck as explanation and action, rather than as a list of departments or activities.

## Reference Structures

### Management policy briefing

Start with the context that changed and the judgment management made. Move through the selected initiatives, their current progress, and the questions that remain unresolved. Give each initiative a clear connection to the main direction so that local updates do not become isolated status reports.

### Corporate strategy meeting

Move from direction to portfolio, resource allocation, execution evidence, and next moves. Keep the same strategic categories across pages so that the audience can see how priorities, investment, performance, and management attention connect.

## Page Grammar

- **Executive status:** show the overall state, the main outcome, the largest variance, and the decision required.
- **Outcome page:** compare target, actual result, prior period, and supplied benchmark on one scale.
- **Driver page:** connect the result to its major causes and show their relative effect.
- **Initiative page:** show objective, current state, milestone, owner, dependency, and next gate in one reading path.
- **Risk page:** connect exposure, trigger, effect, response, and decision date.
- **Portfolio page:** keep businesses, regions, projects, or priorities on common dimensions.
- **Decision page:** state the choice, options, trade-offs, responsible owner, and required timing.
- **Next-actions page:** end with named actions, owners, dependencies, and dates from the supplied material.

## Information Structure

Use structural diagrams for progress, responsibilities, dependencies, risks, and decision flow. Keep status, evidence, interpretation, owner, and next action in stable positions across repeated management pages. Place key values on the chart or table that supports them.

Before delivery, check the title sequence as a management summary. Then confirm that each requested decision has enough context, that every action retains its supplied owner and timing, and that status labels mean the same thing throughout the deck.
