# Business Proposal

## Narrative

Build one clear line of persuasion. Move from tension to possibility, confidence, and action. Give the core claim of each page the strongest visual position, and make every chart, image, diagram, or example support that claim.

Select the sequence that fits the proposal. Common material includes purpose, problem, timing, solution, mechanism, proof, business model, market, plan, people, and the requested action. Use only the parts that help this proposal reach its decision.

## Reference Structures

### Company introduction

Start with the mission and the concrete problem it addresses. Show the product as a working answer, then explain the business, adoption, operating system, people, and culture that make the answer credible. End with a clear invitation, partnership, or request.

### Investor factbook

Start from the market structure and the role the company can own. Explain the business model and operating system before presenting metrics, growth drivers, risks, and the planned use of resources. Keep definitions and metric scope stable so that the argument becomes more credible as detail increases.

## Page Grammar

- **Opening page:** state the proposition and identify the audience or situation that it concerns.
- **Problem page:** make the current friction, cost, or missed opportunity concrete.
- **Why-now page:** connect the opportunity to supplied changes in behavior, technology, regulation, or market structure.
- **Solution page:** show the product, service, or proposal as a direct response to the problem.
- **Mechanism page:** use a structural diagram to show how value is created and delivered.
- **Proof page:** present supplied adoption, performance, customer, financial, or operational facts close to the claim they support.
- **Model page:** show how value, money, resources, and incentives move through the system.
- **Plan page:** connect milestones, dependencies, resource use, and validation gates.
- **People page:** connect each supplied person or partner to the capability the plan requires.
- **Ask page:** state the requested action, what it enables, and what happens next.

## Rhythm and Persuasion

Use section transitions and accent pages when the argument changes emotional or logical phase. Alternate claim-led pages with product, image, diagram, or data-led pages so that proof follows promise. Reuse a small set of motifs and page axes to keep the sequence recognizable without making every page identical.

Before delivery, read the page titles in order and check that each one earns the next. Confirm that the requested action follows from the supplied problem, mechanism, proof, and plan, and that no page repeats a claim without adding a new reason to believe it.
